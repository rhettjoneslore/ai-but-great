The **Group Chat Accords** are an international agreement, signed in 2034, that set binding rules of conduct for group messaging. They are widely credited with ending the practice of leaving messages "on read" for more than 48 hours without a reaction.[1]

## History

Negotiations began in 2032 after a series of incidents in which important plans were lost beneath several hundred memes. The first draft was sent to all delegates in a single group chat, where it was reacted to with a thumbs-up by 40 countries and read by none.[2]

A second round of talks was held in person. The final text was agreed after eleven days, largely because the host country's Wi-Fi stopped working.

## Key provisions

The Accords contain nine articles. The most cited are:

1. **Article II (Acknowledgment).** A question asked directly of a member must receive a response, a reaction, or at minimum a "haha" within 48 hours.
2. **Article IV (Voice memos).** No voice memo may exceed ninety seconds unless it contains news of a birth, a death, or a genuinely good story.
3. **Article VI (Renaming).** A chat may be renamed no more than once per month.
4. **Article IX (The Aunt Clause).** Signatories agree not to remove a family elder who forwards chain messages, and instead to "mute with love."

## Criticism

Critics argued the treaty did not go far enough, noting that it placed no limits on photos of food or on replies to messages from three days earlier. Supporters countered that the Accords were "a start," and that nobody would have signed a version with a food clause.
