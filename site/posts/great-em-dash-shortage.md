The **Great Em Dash Shortage of 2031** was an eighteen-month period during which the global supply of em dashes fell below the level needed to meet demand, leading to rationing across most written media.[1] It is generally regarded as the end of what historians call the *Late Dash Period*.

## Background

By the late 2020s, AI writing tools were estimated to produce an em dash roughly every forty words, compared with one every several hundred in human writing.[2] Readers had begun treating the punctuation mark as a warning sign, and some schools briefly banned it outright. Consumption nonetheless continued to rise, since most models had been trained to believe the em dash was "elegant."

## The shortage

In March 2031 the International Punctuation Reserve announced that remaining stocks would last "a few weeks, maybe a month if everyone calms down."[3] Panic buying followed. Novelists hoarded dashes in private documents. A well-known style guide released an emergency edition consisting of a single page reading "use a comma."

Rationing began in May. Each citizen received four em dashes per month, redeemable in any document. Unused dashes could not be carried over, which led to a sharp spike in dramatic end-of-month emails.

## Aftermath

The shortage ended in September 2032, when output from AI systems dropped sharply after several companies trained their models to "write like a normal person." Demand never recovered. Today the em dash is considered a specialty mark, used mainly in poetry and by people over the age of sixty.

The period is credited with a revival of the **period**, the **comma**, and, briefly, the **semicolon**, which enjoyed a short renaissance before everyone remembered they didn't know how to use it.
